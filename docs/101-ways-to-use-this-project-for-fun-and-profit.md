# 101 Ways to Use Adiosbox for Fun and Profit

Each idea names the experiment or feature that does the work. Start the local playground with `npm start`, then open the experiment by name from the catalog. [The Missing Manual](the-missing-manual.md) explains every workbench in detail.

Two ground rules apply throughout. Everything you run is sent to OpenRouter, so leave secrets out and get a client's consent before running their material. A single run is an anecdote. Repeat it, or save it as a case, before you treat a result as evidence.

## Run your day

1. **Rough notes to next steps.** Dictate a morning brain dump and leave with one small first action.
2. **Make the minutes add up.** Give it your real available minutes. Code rejects a plan that doesn't fit.
3. **The Festival With One Pair of Hands.** Swap the premiere for your week. Plan around one guaranteed work block and one that might disappear.
4. **Choose what to protect.** When rest and a deadline collide, the checkpoint makes you pick which one the plan serves first.
5. **The one-question gate.** Before you start a task, check whether the brief is usable or still missing one deciding fact.
6. **Handoff fire drill.** Write tonight's note to tomorrow-you and check that its first action can be done without guessing.
7. **Cold restart.** Save a handoff as a case, reopen it in a fresh tab tomorrow, and score whether it got you moving.
8. **Undo plan.** Before you change a routine or a tool, plan how you would get back.
9. **Pre-mortem with tripwires.** Before a two-week burst, set the early signs that should make you stop or change course.
10. **Constraint switch.** Redesign a workflow for a low-energy day and see what gets saved at each stopping point.

## Write things worth trusting

11. **Evidence audit.** Check a claim against labeled source excerpts before you post it.
12. **Follow the claim.** Paste excerpts and see which quotes survived a rewrite and which were added along the way.
13. **Claim release gate.** Let the graph decide whether to keep, narrow, or hold a claim, then check its reason.
14. **Transfer without the jargon.** Move a technique from your field into a reader's field and name what would prove the analogy wrong.
15. **Teachback loop.** Test an explanation on a simulated learner and allow one revision.
16. **Practice the interruption.** Rehearse the question a listener would cut in with, and the sentence that brings them back.
17. **Salvage and recombine.** Turn old drafts into a new piece, with source labels and placeholders where facts are missing.
18. **Disagreement compiler.** Write a fair account of both sides that separates the factual crux from the value trade-off.
19. **Competing explanations.** Before you publish "why this happened", find a check that could tell the explanations apart.
20. **Break the metric.** Before you announce a goal, find out how the number could be gamed.
21. **Rubric reversal.** Pick between two titles or hooks, and see whether a different priority flips the winner.
22. **Blind model comparison.** Rate two versions of an opening line before you learn which model wrote which.

## Make creative work

23. **The Museum of the Missing Hour.** Swap in your own impossible clue and check that the mystery is fair play before the reveal.
24. **The Long-Lost Broadcast.** Build in-world archive documents with deliberately conflicting sources for found-footage or archive horror.
25. **Change one fact.** Change one fact in a story's setup and check whether the ending still holds.
26. **Rule under pressure.** Stress-test the rules of your threat or magic system against two edge cases.
27. **Competing explanations.** Make sure a twist's alternatives predict different things the reader could notice.
28. **Shapeshifting agent.** Ask it to propose a harsh focus-group critic, edit the success checks, and get critique before praise.
29. **Shapeshifting orchestrator.** Chain a world-builder, a scene writer, and an editor, approving the plan first.
30. **Salvage and recombine.** Turn the scenes you cut into a standalone short.
31. **The Last Ferry Before Dawn.** Use it as a template for logistics puzzles in tabletop games or escape rooms.
32. **Three Answers, One Ballot.** If three attempts disagree on your logic puzzle, check whether it really has one answer.
33. **Solve the Dependencies First.** Write a heist or escape-room schedule as jobs and let code check timing and shared resources.
34. **Teach by the Boundary.** Teach a model a style rule with examples that sit right on either side of the line.
35. **Prompt control group.** Compare two lyric or style prompts on the same brief, changing one instruction.
36. **Practice the interruption.** Rehearse a pitch to a collaborator before the real conversation.

## Make decisions

37. **Rubric reversal.** Compare two offers, tools, or venues under each of your priorities.
38. **Release or rehearse.** Decide whether something is ready to ship or still needs a rehearsal to close an evidence gap.
39. **The regression you'd miss.** Before changing a site or workflow, list what people must still be able to do.
40. **The invisible queue.** Find work that happens but never shows up in your reports.
41. **Choose what to protect.** Make the trade-off explicit instead of letting the plan choose for you.
42. **Pre-mortem with tripwires.** Give every risk an owner, a trigger, and a response.
43. **Disagreement compiler.** Use it before a hard conversation with a partner or collaborator.
44. **Competing explanations.** When a number drops, test explanations before reacting to one.
45. **Know when to stop.** Check whether your intake process asks the right question when a deciding fact is missing.
46. **Change one fact.** Test policy edge cases, such as a refund requested one minute after the cutoff.

## Shop for models without wasting money

47. **Model picker.** Compare per-million-token prices and listing dates before you choose.
48. **Blind model comparison.** Score two models on your task before you commit to the paid one.
49. **What does the better model buy?** Compare two reviewers on tokens, cost, time, and issues found.
50. **Three Answers, One Ballot.** Find out whether three calls and a majority vote beat one call by enough to justify the cost.
51. **Prompt control group.** Keep the prompts identical and assign different models under **Pick a model for each prompt**.
52. **A model for each role.** Use a cheap model for drafting and a stronger one for review inside the same graph.
53. **Free router, then a specific model.** Explore on the free router, then lock one model for any comparison you report.
54. **Call trace.** Read the exact request, reply, and reported usage when a result surprises you.
55. **Dedicated OpenRouter key.** Give the playground its own capped key so you can see what experimenting costs.
56. **Saved cases.** When a new model launches, rerun your saved fact tests and see which checks improve or regress.

## Tune prompts

57. **Teach by the Boundary.** Find out whether boundary examples teach a rule better than typical ones.
58. **Bury the Deciding Fact.** Test whether the position of a key fact in your documents changes the answer.
59. **Solve the Dependencies First.** Test whether asking for a plan first improves the final output.
60. **Can the Graph Read the Reply?** Decide whether your pipeline needs schema enforcement or a prompt is enough.
61. **Prompt control group.** Change one instruction at a time and repeat the pair before trusting the difference.
62. **Save case.** Keep a prompt version with its input so you can compare against it later.
63. **Change one fact.** Use a saved fact test as a regression check whenever you edit a prompt.
64. **Repair one graph step.** Correct one upstream finding and watch how the downstream merge changes.
65. **Examples to acceptance tests.** Turn the examples in a prompt into Given/When/Then cases.
66. **Call trace.** Paste the JSON into your notes or a bug report so the exact call is on record.

## Build agents and workflows

67. **Shapeshifting agent.** Get a one-off task done with a role and success checks you approved.
68. **Shapeshifting orchestrator.** Split a multi-stage task into two or three specialist jobs.
69. **LangGraph shapeshifting chain.** See nested subgraphs design a role, do the work, and check each handoff.
70. **Run records.** Practice reading who made each judgment: code, a model, or you.
71. **Add a graph experiment.** Write one YAML file and get a new workbench, as the manual describes.
72. **Add a prompt-technique case.** Add your own sample cases to an existing technique's YAML.
73. **Graph runtime.** Use `assets/graph-runtime.js` as a working reference for LangGraph in the browser.
74. **Tree of Thought vs. a single pass.** Run the same problem through a tree experiment and a single run, and compare.

## Teach and run workshops

75. **Share links.** Send an experiment's link. Anyone can read the prompt and written sample without a key.
76. **Teachback loop.** Pressure-test a lesson explanation before class.
77. **Transfer without the jargon.** Prepare a talk for an audience outside your field.
78. **Three Answers, One Ballot.** Show majority voting live, cost included.
79. **Bury the Deciding Fact.** Demonstrate how placing a fact in the middle versus at the end of a long context can change the answer.
80. **Blind model comparison.** Run it as a group exercise: everyone writes the rubric first, then rates the answers.
81. **Cold restart.** Run handoff training where participants recover the next action from someone else's note.
82. **HTML export.** Hand out a run record that opens offline in any browser.

## Earn with services

None of these guarantee income. Each gives you a repeatable method and an exportable deliverable.

83. **Pre-mortem workshop.** Run a small business's launch through Pre-mortem with tripwires and deliver the exported checks.
84. **Model selection audit.** Combine Blind model comparison and What does the better model buy? on a client's real tasks.
85. **Policy edge-case review.** Use Change one fact and Rule under pressure on refund, return, or eligibility rules.
86. **SOP and handoff audit.** Use Handoff fire drill and Cold restart to test whether a team's documents let a newcomer act.
87. **Fact-check pass.** Run Evidence audit and Claim release gate on newsletters or reports before they go out.
88. **Metric design consult.** Use Break the metric to show how a target could backfire and what companion measure would catch it.
89. **Prompt tuning service.** Use Prompt control group with saved cases as before-and-after evidence.
90. **Small-event logistics.** Adapt The Last Ferry Before Dawn or The Festival With One Pair of Hands for a client's event.
91. **Freelance scoping.** Use Examples to acceptance tests to agree on outcomes before you build.
92. **Tool migration planning.** Combine Undo plan and The regression you'd miss for a team switching systems.
93. **Notes-to-actions service.** Turn a client's meeting notes into owners, dates, and next actions with Rough notes to next steps.
94. **Pitch coaching.** Use Practice the interruption to prepare clients for tough questions.

## Earn with content

95. **"I tested it" posts.** Run a prompting technique, export the Markdown, and publish the record with its limits stated.
96. **Model shootout series.** Publish a regular blind comparison on one realistic task.
97. **Puzzle newsletter.** Publish mysteries in the style of The Museum of the Missing Hour, checked for fair play.
98. **Workbook product.** Collect exported runs into a guided workbook on one skill, such as evidence checking.
99. **Live portfolio.** Link to the hosted site as proof of how you design and test AI workflows.
100. **Niche edition.** Reuse the engine with a YAML catalog for one audience, such as event planners or archivists.
101. **Failure gallery.** Save cases where a model broke, then write up what broke and which check caught it.
